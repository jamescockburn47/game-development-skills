# Choose shots that explain the game

Start by playing or observing a complete small action. List two to four observable
experiences worth showing, with evidence they exist in this build. A trailer should
answer what the player does, why it is interesting, and how this game feels.
Use the creator's supplied description; captions may describe observed mechanics.

## A compact editorial sequence

Choose an opening with a readable subject and action within the first few seconds.
Follow with enough context to understand the place or task, then an escalating
sequence of distinct experiences and a clear ending. An uncut walkthrough instead
needs a navigable route and honest continuity; do not turn it into a montage.

Useful shot purposes include:

| Purpose | What the viewer should see |
|---|---|
| Orient | Who/where the player is and what matters nearby |
| Act | A real move, choice, interaction or skill being used |
| Consequence | A changed board, hit reaction, earned reward or changed world |
| Contrast | A meaningfully different space, challenge, scale or approach |
| Conclude | A resolved moment or accurate next step, held long enough to read |

Include a player's view even when cinematic cameras are available. One beautiful
establishing shot can set tone; repeated identical orbits rarely explain play.
Do not substitute manually spawned effects for the mechanic a caption promises.
Staging initial resources may demonstrate spending them, but cannot prove earning them.

## Adapt composition to the game

- **2D / pixel art:** preserve intended pixel scaling, board edges and active targets.
  Keep text crisp; avoid smooth zooms that shimmer or obscure the whole board.
- **Strategy / simulation:** show the choice and its visible consequence. Give menus
  time to read; keep the relevant panel and affected world region in the same sequence.
- **Action / racing / platforming:** show approach, contact and recovery. Track the
  subject without hiding timing, hit reactions, landing or the destination.
- **Exploration / 3D worlds:** use distinct places and activities. Establish scale,
  then move closer. Inspect occlusion throughout the camera path, not only its endpoints.
- **Narrative / text games:** hold dialogue and choices for realistic reading time;
  protect spoiler boundaries. Avoid rapid montage as a substitute for understanding.
- **Mobile / portrait:** compose in the actual aspect ratio and respect device safe
  areas. Show touch feedback only when accurate and useful. Check on a phone-sized view.
- **VR / unusual displays:** choose a comfortable spectator view or supported format,
  label it accurately and verify playback. A shaky eye view is not the only option.

Keep action and captions apart. Preserve useful HUD; hide debug chrome only when it
does not remove information central to understanding the mechanic. A caption cannot
repair an invisible subject. Check text contrast and dwell time at delivery size.
Avoid gratuitous flashing, abrupt camera swings and sped-up motion presented as normal.

## Scout before committing

For each candidate, inspect a beginning, peak and ending plus a short moving preview.
Select against: subject visible; mechanic understandable; meaningful outcome;
stable framing; distinct contribution; sound and captions feasible. Keep the verdict
as a sentence, not an unexplained numerical score. Use another view or shorter camera
move if terrain, crowds or particles repeatedly obscure the subject.

Use target-relative camera heights and deliberate paths where appropriate. Repeatedly
sampling rough terrain for camera height can cause stepping. A subject-centred orbit
must still be checked for walls, foreground objects and flattened silhouettes.
Warm up streaming, shadows, temporal antialiasing and exposure before recording.
Reset precipitation and particles between locations so the previous shot cannot bleed in.

Choose variety because it explains the game, not to advertise unimplemented breadth.
Retain the actual action result where a claim depends on it: pressing "buy" is weaker
evidence than seeing money fall and the purchased item appear. Final selection requires
looking at the encoded shot; event logs and camera geometry cannot judge its appearance.
