# Evidence probes

Read this when a world claim depends on real traversal, streaming, pacing, visual
output or interaction between several systems.

## Frame the claim

Record:

- player-facing claim and consequence tier;
- ordinary entry state and critical journey;
- hardest uncertain mechanism;
- pass/fail observation;
- plausible false positive;
- fixture and acceleration budget;
- artifacts to retain.
- evidence type and lineage: intended design, inspected code, recorded run or fresh
  reproduction; copied policy, forks and shared datasets do not count as independent
  corroboration.

A representative slice should be small enough to repeat and broad enough to cross the
boundaries at issue. It might include leaving a safe area, selecting between two
routes, streaming through a boundary, interacting with a systemic actor, suffering a
setback, recovering, saving and returning. Change the shape when those beats are not
part of the game.

## Match the probe to the claim

Use deterministic tests for spatial transforms, path constraints, quantities, state
transitions, event eligibility, duplicate rejection and save equivalence. Include a
mutation or deliberately wrong fixture that the assertion rejects.

Use the real coordinator and ordinary controls when the claim depends on first-use
guidance, timing, streaming order, AI interaction or persistence wiring. Verify the
consequence, not only that a button rendered or an event count increased.

Use a controlled comparison when assessing density, route choice, faction response,
economic pressure, aid or recovery. Change one material variable and compare outcomes.
Do not infer causation from two runs that also changed seed, equipment and route.

Inspect rendered output for navigation and world readability. Use the normal camera
and representative weather, lighting, crowding and LOD transitions. State the visual
criteria before inspection: landmark recognisability, hazard notice, target identity,
interface obstruction or transition continuity.

Profile representative loads after behavior is correct. Report frame-time distribution
or meaningful worst cases rather than a single average. Separate CPU simulation,
rendering, streaming and save costs when the remedy depends on the cause.

## Keep accelerated evidence honest

Fixed-step acceleration can establish simulation-time pacing and deterministic
outcomes. It cannot establish perceived waiting, input feel, animation smoothness or
human comprehension. Teleportation can establish a destination state, not the route.
Granted inventory can test a transaction, not acquisition. Seeded populations can
test interaction, not natural encounter frequency.

Do not invent a player ability to create the starting state. If an injured animal,
destroyed structure or other required condition has no established acquisition path,
use a supplied save or an explicitly injected fixture for the isolated check. Keep
that fixture separate from any claim about the ordinary gameplay journey.

Label active simulation time, wall time and fictional calendar time separately. Keep
ordinary hazards and competing systems enabled unless the probe explicitly isolates
one mechanism; disclose every isolation.

When a surprising failure occurs, distinguish product behavior from harness behavior
with one discriminating rerun or lower-layer check. Do not turn a stale selector,
missed readiness wait or edited development reload into a balance conclusion.

## Evidence record

Preserve a compact record:

```markdown
## Claim and verdict
Pass, fail or partial, with the player consequence.

## Method
Build/revision, entry state, controls, seed, active and wall time, acceleration,
fixtures, and systems intentionally left active.

## Observations
Raw quantities, state transitions, screenshots or traces, including the failed
counterexample.

## Limits
What the probe cannot establish and which part of the intended journey was bypassed.

## Revision
Changed hypothesis, smallest correction, rerun result, and retained risk.
Dependent work or claims invalidated or superseded by a failed central mechanism.
```

Stop after one evidence-led revision unless the result changes the hypothesis or the
unresolved risk justifies another experiment. A failed central mechanism blocks a
completion claim; it does not authorize reducing the user's intended outcome.
